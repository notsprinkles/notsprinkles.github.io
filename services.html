Services

Human-factor security that makes people harder to exploit—without turning them paranoid.

Most breaches don’t start with “hacking.” They start with a human moment: trust, urgency, ambiguity, authority, helpfulness, or social pressure. I help organizations identify those moments, reduce them, and train teams to respond safely with confidence.

What I’m best at

  • Human Attack Surface assessment: where normal behavior creates unintended access or exposure
  • Role-aware training: what employees actually face (not generic “don’t click” slides)
  • Vendor/third-party risk: onsite + remote behaviors that collapse physical boundaries
  • Clear refusal language: how to say “no” safely under authority/urgency pressure
  • Policy-to-reality bridging: making rules usable in messy real-world moments

Core offerings

1) Human Attack Surface Assessment

A structured, human-layer risk assessment focused on how trust, ambiguity, norms, and role dynamics create exposure—often without any malicious intent. Output is practical: what’s happening, why it’s happening, and what to change.

  • Trust pathways (introductions, “trusted adjacent” requests, community/company boundary crossing)
  • Authority ambiguity (implied authority, unclear policy references, “just a quick question” probes)
  • Oversharing & aggregation risk (harmless bits that become a profile)
  • Access normalization (shared secrets, “temporary” exceptions becoming permanent)
  • Norm drift (how teams slide from “safe” → “questionable” without noticing)
  • Vendor and onsite digital behavior (video calls, photos, screen sharing in controlled spaces)

2) Demonstration Workshops (Authorized & Controlled)

If you want a demonstration component, it’s done as a learning exercise—not a “gotcha.” Demonstrations are pre-scoped, consent-based, and designed to reveal failure modes safely.

  • No real system access
  • No credential collection
  • No targeting or naming individuals
  • Focus is on patterns: authority cues, urgency framing, ambiguity, and social pressure

3) Employee Training & Role-Based Coaching

Training that respects reality: people are busy, helpful, and often operating under pressure. The goal is not fear—it's safe clarity.

  • All-hands training: “How social engineering actually works in modern teams”
  • Manager training: “How to set norms that reduce risk without slowing work”
  • IT / Helpdesk training: “Authority + urgency: the highest-frequency pressure points”
  • Exec/admin training: “High-trust roles & targeted requests”
  • Vendor briefings: “Onsite digital behavior + physical boundary protection”

Deliverables

  • Human Attack Surface Findings Report (clear risk narratives + prioritized fixes)
  • Top 10 “Risk Moments” list tailored to your workflows
  • Refusal language playbook (what to say when you’re pressured)
  • Manager checklist (how to reduce ambiguity + norm drift)
  • Vendor onboarding insert (plain-language, enforceable behaviors)
  • Training deck + facilitator notes for repeatable internal rollout

What this is not

  • No unauthorized testing
  • No “break-in” stunts
  • No publishing company details
  • No shaming employees for being human
  • No tactical instructions for wrongdoing

Ideal clients

Organizations with high trust, fast decisions, and lots of collaboration—where social engineering risk is real because people are moving quickly.

  • Remote-first and hybrid teams
  • Healthcare / clinics / facilities with vendors onsite
  • Tech companies and startups
  • Education and public-facing organizations
  • Communities with shared access and role-based responsibility

How an engagement works

  1. Scope & goals (30–60 min): who, what, and what “success” means
  2. Discovery: policies, workflows, and common pressure points
  3. Assessment: human-layer risk mapping + pattern extraction
  4. Readout: prioritized findings + quick wins + longer-term fixes
  5. Training: tailored sessions + role-specific refusal language

FAQ

Will employees be embarrassed?

No. This work is designed to reduce risk without blame. Findings focus on systems, incentives, ambiguity, and norms—not on individuals.

Can this be tailored to our industry?

Yes. The patterns are universal, but the triggers are industry-specific.
