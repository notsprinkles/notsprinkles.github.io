HAS-008

Third-Party Digital Behavior and Physical Boundary Collapse

Framework

Human Attack Surface (HAS)

Context (abstracted)

Environment: controlled physical facility
Participants: third-party vendor present onsite
Medium: live video communication with a trusted remote contact
Condition: no malicious intent; no explicit policy reminder at point of action

Hypothesis

When third-party vendors are not explicitly trained on digital behavior within physical facilities, digital trust can unintentionally override physical security awareness. The result is incidental exposure of sensitive environments without deliberate access or malicious intent.

Example (anonymized)

A third-party vendor performing routine work inside a controlled facility engaged in a live video call with a trusted remote contact. The call was informal and unrelated to the facility itself.

While moving through the space, the vendor continued the call without awareness that the camera captured operational areas and contextual details of the environment. No systems were accessed, no credentials were shared, and no policy was knowingly violated.

The exposure occurred solely through visual and contextual leakage — enabled by familiarity, trust, and the absence of explicit guidance regarding video communication inside the facility.

Failure Mode

The failure did not occur at the technical or access-control layer. It occurred at the boundary between digital behavior and physical space. Digital trust relationships were unconsciously extended into a physical environment that relied on assumed vigilance.

Root Causes

  • Third-party vendors not embedded in internal security culture
  • No explicit prohibition or guidance regarding live video calls onsite
  • Assumption that “common sense” would prevent exposure
  • False perception that being on a call is a neutral or harmless activity

Human Factors Identified

Familiarity bias • trust transfer • task fixation • environmental blindness • normalization of video communication • boundary misalignment

Systemic Failure Mode

Physical security controls relied on implicit behavioral expectations rather than explicit training. Digital behaviors were not included in the threat model for third-party presence, leaving a gap between policy intent and real-world action.

Defensive Implications

Organizations should treat digital behavior as part of physical security. Third-party onboarding must explicitly address video calls, screen sharing, and remote communication while onsite.

Simple controls — such as clear signage, vendor briefings, and explicit “no video” zones — significantly reduce this class of exposure. Assumed vigilance is not a control; training is.

Ethical Notes

This case is anonymized and presented solely to illustrate a class of systemic failure. No individuals or organizations are identified. The intent is preventative education, not attribution or critique.
