Methodology & Ethics

The Human Attack Surface (HAS) framework documents how human behavior, cognition, environment, and trust dynamics create security risk in real systems. This work is experience-driven, observational, and defensive in intent.

Purpose

HAS exists to make human-centered failure modes visible without turning them into “how-to” guidance. I publish my own experiments and lived observations to support defensive awareness, training, and better design.

What this research is

HAS research is observational, experience-driven, and systemic. It focuses on conditions (context, fatigue, authority, familiarity, accessibility reliance, and environmental leakage) rather than blaming individuals.

What this research is not

This is not penetration testing, credential harvesting, or an exploitation guide. No identifying details, timelines, or organization-specific markers are published.

Experiment scope

Experiments may involve:

  • Trust formation and voluntary disclosure patterns
  • Environmental leakage (visual/audio/contextual)
  • Cognitive load, fatigue, and urgency dynamics
  • Authority-adjacent or parasocial trust cues
  • Accessibility and assistive-technology reliance

Documentation format

Each experiment is recorded as:

  • Experiment ID (HAS-001, HAS-002…)
  • Abstracted context (industry type, medium, relationship state)
  • Hypothesis
  • Observations (neutral, factual)
  • Trigger conditions
  • Human factors identified
  • Systemic failure mode
  • Defensive implications
  • Ethical notes

Ethical boundaries

  • No real names or identifiers
  • No company attribution
  • No step-by-step exploitation instructions
  • No harm intent; defensive purpose only

If a detail makes an incident uniquely identifiable, it does not get published.

All content reflects my own observations and experiences and is intended solely for defensive education.
